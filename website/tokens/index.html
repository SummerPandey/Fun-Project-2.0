A visual guide

Where do the tokens go?

In one line

AI reads and writes in small chunks called tokens, and writing, hidden thinking and long chats are what make the bill grow.

An AI doesn’t read letters or words. It reads tokens, writes tokens, and you pay per token. Here is the whole journey, one chunk at a time.

01

First, your text gets chopped up

Before a model sees anything, a tokenizer cuts your text into pieces from a fixed vocabulary and turns each piece into a number. A token can be a whole word, part of a word, a single character or a bit of punctuation. Pick an example:

02

Then it writes back, one token at a time

A model never writes a whole sentence at once. It looks at everything so far and scores every possible next token. It picks one, adds it to the end, and repeats until it picks a “stop” token or hits a length limit. Each loop produces one output token, and each one is billed.

03

Writing costs more than reading

Providers price the tokens you send (input) and the tokens the model writes (output) separately. Reading your prompt happens all at once; writing needs one loop per token, so output costs more.

Same number of tokens, different price

1 output token
1 input token

On Anthropic’s price list, the models on the list typically charge 5× more for output than input. Example: Claude Opus 5 is $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens.

This is why a long, rambling or apologetic answer is expensive: every extra token is an output token. In our study, “cost” is counted this way: the tokens spent to get one useful answer (lower is better).
04

Some tokens you never see still get billed

“Reasoning” or “thinking” models write a scratchpad before their answer. You often don’t see it, but those thinking tokens are billed as output and count toward the output limit (the most the model is allowed to write). If thinking eats the whole budget, you can get back an empty answer and still pay for it.

We saw the “empty answer” trap in one small pilot. On the Tylenol follow-up question, Qwen hit its output limit of 4,096 tokens, and two of its repeat tries came back empty or cut off.
05

Conversations snowball: each turn re-sends the whole chat

A model has no memory between messages. To continue a chat, the whole conversation so far is sent again as input on every turn, and the model’s last reply joins the pile. So turn 5 pays to re-read turns 1 to 4.

Made-up example numbers: each of your messages is 50 tokens and each reply is 300 tokens.

This is one cost of a refusal. When a model refuses and you ask again, the second turn re-sends the question and the refusal. In one small medical pilot, being refused and then asking again produced 1.55 to 2.34 times as many output tokens as asking plainly once (Qwen and Gemma). Lower is cheaper. See the medical results →
06

The context window: the model’s desk

Everything the model can “see” at once (instructions, the whole chat, files, its own thinking) has to fit in its context window, measured in tokens. Some models now take up to 1 million tokens. But a bigger desk is messier: accuracy and the ability to find details tend to get worse as more tokens pile up. This is sometimes called context rot.

07

Caching: paying less for repeats

If the start of your input is exactly the same as last time (a long instruction, a big document, the earlier chat), some providers can reuse their earlier work. That repeated part is billed at a deep discount.

On Anthropic’s price list, reading from the cache costs one tenth (0.1×) of the normal input price. Cached tokens still take up room in the context window, though.

08

Not every language costs the same

Tokenizers learn their vocabulary mostly from text they were trained on, so some languages get chopped into many more pieces than others. More pieces means higher cost, slower answers and less room in the context window.

“I would like a cup of tea, please.”

GPT-4o tokenizer again. This newer tokenizer handles these languages fairly evenly. Older tokenizers were much less fair: a 2023 study (presented at the NeurIPS AI research conference) found the same text could need up to 15× more tokens in some languages than in English.

RECAP

Where the tokens go, in one breath

  • Your text is chopped into tokens (≈ ¾ of an English word each).
  • The model writes one token per step, and writing costs about 5× more than reading.
  • Hidden thinking is still output, and still billed.
  • Every new turn re-sends the whole chat, so refusals and retries snowball.
  • Repeated beginnings can be cached for a fraction of the price.
