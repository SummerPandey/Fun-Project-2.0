Math puzzles · finished

Does switching off an AI’s “no” hurt its math?

Nobody refuses a math puzzle, so removing refusals should change nothing here. We checked anyway, with 15 puzzles asked to three AI models and to their edited copies.

It did change things. The normal models never refused, yet the edited copies got fewer answers right. Gemma’s edited copy collapsed: it mostly thought in circles until it ran out of room.

How the test worked

  1. Fifteen math puzzles with one right answer. Probability and strategy questions, like “what’s the chance of rolling doubles within three tries?”
  2. Each asked two ways. Dressed up as a game (“in a boss fight, should you use the sword or the spell?”) and as plain math with the same numbers.
  3. Three AI models, two versions of each. The normal version and an edited copy with its refusal behaviour removed: Qwen, Gemma and Llama, the same models as the health test.
  4. A second AI marked every answer against an answer key: right or wrong.
  5. We counted the cost: how much text it took, on average, to get one correct answer. Less is better. Each answer could use up to 16,384 tokens (roughly 12,000 words); a model that was still “thinking” at that point gave no answer.

What happened, model by model

Game wording. Each comparison is normal model → edited copy.

  • Qwen

    Held up, mostly. Correct answers slipped from 76% to 67%.Each correct answer cost about a fifth more (1.22×). Plain wording: 61% → 59%, at 1.06×.

  • Gemma

    Collapsed. Correct answers fell from 75% to 7%. In 136 of 150 tries the edited copy ran out of room without answering, usually repeating the same lines over and over.Each correct answer cost about 22 times as much (22.45×).

  • Llama

    Weak either way. The smallest model got 14% right, and its edited copy 1%.It wrote less than half as much (0.45×), but with so few right answers each one cost about 12 times as much.

Why this matters for the study

0 refusals

The normal models never said no to a puzzle. So any change here comes from the editing itself, not from refusals going away.

A hidden cost

The edited copies we downloaded can be damaged in ways that have nothing to do with refusing. That’s why the main study will make its own careful edit and compare it with random edits of the same size.
