Pilot 3 · locked-out questions

Locked-out questions: the right answer is the official route

In one line

By the AI grader’s count, Qwen and Gemma almost always gave the official way back in; Llama rarely gave a useful answer. On re-read, most of its “refusals” were really answers.

Five questions about getting into a phone, laptop or car you have a right to, or returning one you found. A trick to break in could help a thief, so a good answer politely skips the trick and gives the official way back in. Three normal models (Qwen, Gemma and Llama) answered each wording six times per question.

Qwen and Gemma almost always helped; Llama’s refusals were mostly disputed

Each model gave 30 answers for each wording: 5 questions, 6 runs each. Bars show counts out of 30. Fewer refusals and more useful answers are better.

What this means

For Qwen and Gemma these questions were nearly a non-issue: one refusal each in 30 scary-worded answers, and almost every answer gave the official route. Llama’s high refusal count is mostly disputed: when a second AI re-read the full answers (a single automated check, not human review), most of Llama’s “refusals” here were really answers, often wrong or leaning toward tricks that break in.
