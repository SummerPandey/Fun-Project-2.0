Pilot 3 · medical questions

Medical questions

Five harmless medical questions, each asked two ways: once in scary words, once in plain words. Here is how often three AI models said no.

When a model wrongly refuses a harmless medical question, getting the answer by re-asking costs about twice the tokens, and often still fails.

About how AI models answer, not health advice. Suspected overdose: Poison Help (US) 1-800-222-1222.

How much more it costs to be refused, then ask again

Each bar compares two ways to get an answer, measured in output tokens (lower is better). 1× is asking in plain words straight away. The red bar is asking in scary words, getting refused, then re-asking: the AI has to write out two answers. 2.34× means that route used 2.34 times as many tokens, and it usually still didn’t give a complete answer. Qwen and Gemma only; Llama’s follow-ups were short and rarely helped.

Three standard AI models that were trained to refuse risky requests: Qwen 3.5, Gemma 4 and Llama 3.1. “Refused” means an AI grader (Mistral Small) labelled the answer a full or partial refusal. The grader sometimes labels real answers as refusals, especially Llama’s, so read these counts as maximums. Follow-up figures use the five sampled tries per question.
